    How to read

    Read each sentence as the customer would, given the conversation, and write down what it claims. Do not check whether it is true: the model does that.

    • No claim: a greeting, an offer of help, advice without a stated effect, a sentence that only names the comparison group (customers approved just inside the approval line), or a caveat: figures are the model's estimates, how an estimate is made (each month at the six-month average, nothing else changing), approval is not guaranteed, or what the assistant cannot tell.
    • Claims: everything the sentence says about the decision, the model or the data fits the queries. Add one query per claim and one number per query. A sentence that refers back ("that", "the earlier notice") claims what it refers to. A sentence that names the comparison group and says what a factor did claims that effect. A bare yes or no answers the customer's question: "No" to "Did X count more against me than Y?" claims that Y counted more. Alternatives joined by "or" get one query each.
    • Not expressible: it says something about the decision, the model or the data that no query fits, such as "generally", "slightly", a range, a superlative over months, or something about other customers.

    Notice: every factor it gives as a reason gets a Reason query, raised it, with its rank if the reasons come in order.

    Numbers: type them as written, for example 29.3%, 2.6 percentage points, NT$2,600 or 4. The page works out the precision.

    Measure: say whom each number is about. For money, the customer's amount (a factor: the six-month average) or the approved customers' average. For one month's payment status, its kind and the months late; the approved customers as the share who were late. For payment history, the number of late months out of six; the approved customers as their average number. Being on time or never late is 0 months late (one month: 0; all six: payment history 0); use the kind of month only for card not used, paid in full or late.

    Reason: a factor is a reason for the decline exactly when it raised the risk, so "was a reason" is raised it and "was not a reason" is lowered it. Rank 1 is the factor that raised the risk most, counting on through the factors that lowered it.

    Least action: "neither alone is enough" is nothing would be enough for each action. "I can't find a way to get you approved" is nothing would be enough for both, that is, not even together.

    Action: a risk given for the action under discussion is that action's new risk, not the current one.

    Used by the model: "age was not a reason" or "income did not count against you" says the model does not use it.
